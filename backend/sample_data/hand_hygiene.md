# Hand Hygiene Protocol

## Purpose
Hand hygiene is the single most effective measure to prevent healthcare-associated infections. This protocol applies to all clinical staff during direct patient care.

## The Five Moments for Hand Hygiene
1. Before touching a patient
2. Before clean or aseptic procedures
3. After body fluid exposure risk
4. After touching a patient
5. After touching the patient's surroundings

## Alcohol-Based Hand Rub (ABHR)
- Apply a palmful of ABHR (follow product label for volume).
- Rub hands covering all surfaces, including thumbs, fingertips, and between fingers.
- Continue until hands are completely dry, approximately 20-30 seconds.
- ABHR is the preferred method for routinely decontaminating hands when they are not visibly soiled.

## Handwashing with Soap and Water
- Wet hands with water and apply enough soap to cover all surfaces.
- Rub hands together for at least 40-60 seconds total.
- Rinse thoroughly and dry with a single-use towel.
- Use soap and water when hands are visibly soiled, after caring for patients with Clostridioides difficile or norovirus, and before eating.

## Nail and Jewelry Guidance
- Fingernails should be kept short (tips less than 0.5 cm).
- Artificial nails and extenders are prohibited for staff performing direct care.
- Rings should be limited to a plain band during clinical duties.

## Glove Use
- Wearing gloves does not replace hand hygiene.
- Perform hand hygiene immediately after glove removal.
- Change gloves between tasks and procedures on the same patient.
