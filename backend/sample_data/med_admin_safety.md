# Medication Administration Safety Reference

## The Rights of Medication Administration
Staff should verify, at minimum: right patient, right drug, right dose, right route, right time, right documentation, and right reason.

## Patient Identification
- Verify identity using at least two identifiers (full name and date of birth).
- Never rely solely on room or bed number.

## High-Alert Medications
High-alert medications require an independent double-check by a second qualified clinician before administration. Common examples include:
- Insulins
- Anticoagulants (heparin, direct oral anticoagulants)
- Opioids and other sedatives
- Concentrated electrolytes (potassium chloride, hypertonic saline)
- Chemotherapy agents

## Independent Double-Check Procedure
1. Each clinician independently verifies the drug, concentration, dose calculation, route, rate, and patient against the original order.
2. Checks are performed separately; clinicians must not share their conclusions before both checks are complete.

## Documentation
- Document administration immediately after giving the medication, never before.
- Record the administered dose, route, site (when applicable), and time.
- Document any held or omitted doses along with the reason.

## Missed Dose Escalation
If a dose is missed, notify the prescriber per unit policy and document the notification. Do not double subsequent doses unless explicitly directed by the prescriber.
