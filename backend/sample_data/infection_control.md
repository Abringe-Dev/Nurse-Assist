# Infection Prevention and Control — Isolation Precautions

## Standard Precautions
Standard precautions apply to all patients. They include hand hygiene, use of personal protective equipment (PPE) when anticipating exposure to blood or body fluids, respiratory hygiene, and safe injection practices.

## Hand Hygiene in Isolation
- Perform hand hygiene before donning and after doffing PPE.
- When caring for patients on contact precautions for Clostridioides difficile or norovirus, use soap and water rather than alcohol-based rub, as spores are not reliably killed by alcohol.

## Transmission-Based Precautions
### Contact Precautions
- Indicated for known or suspected infections transmitted by direct or indirect contact, e.g., MRSA, VRE, C. difficile.
- Place patient in a single room when available.
- Wear gloves and gown on entry; don before room entry, doff before exit.
- Dedicate non-critical equipment to the patient.

### Droplet Precautions
- For pathogens transmitted by respiratory droplets, e.g., influenza, pertussis, COVID-19 (when not aerosol-generating).
- Wear a surgical mask within 3 feet of the patient.
- Patient should wear a mask when transported.

### Airborne Precautions
- For pathogens transmitted via airborne route, e.g., tuberculosis, measles, varicella.
- Place patient in an airborne infection isolation room (AIIR) with negative pressure.
- Wear a fit-tested N95 respirator or higher level of protection.
- Limit transport to essential purposes.

## Personal Protective Equipment Sequence
### Donning (Putting On)
1. Hand hygiene.
2. Gown — fully cover torso, tie at neck and waist.
3. Mask or respirator — secure ties, perform seal check for N95.
4. Goggles or face shield.
5. Gloves — extend over gown cuffs.

### Doffing (Removing)
1. Gloves — grasp outside, peel off, discard.
2. Goggles/face shield — handle by headband.
3. Gown — peel off, turn inside out, discard.
4. Mask/respirator — do not touch front, remove by ties.
5. Hand hygiene after each step where indicated and always at the end.

## Environmental Cleaning
- Clean and disinfect high-touch surfaces daily and upon discharge.
- Use an EPA-registered disinfectant effective against the target organism.
- For C. difficile, use a sporicidal agent (e.g., bleach-based).

## Linen and Waste
- Handle linen to avoid agitation and aerosolization.
- Bag waste at the point of care; for airborne pathogens, follow facility policy for regulated medical waste.

## Patient Placement and Cohorting
- Prioritize single rooms for patients requiring transmission-based precautions.
- When single rooms are unavailable, cohort patients with the same infection, maintaining spatial separation and dedicated staffing when possible.

## Duration of Precautions
- Continue precautions for the duration of hospitalization or as specified by infection control policy.
- Consult infection control for discontinuation criteria (e.g., two consecutive negative tests for C. difficile).

## Documentation
- Document the type of precautions, initiation date/time, patient education provided, and PPE compliance observations.
